# Product Requirements Document
## Satellite and Weather Based Verification Layer for PMFBY Crop Insurance Claims
**Smart India Hackathon 2026**

### 1. Problem Statement
The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana, PMFBY, is India's main crop insurance scheme, and it has a well documented gaming problem. Farmers, or in some cases intermediaries working with farmers, file claims for crop loss that did not actually happen, or the same physical plot gets claimed multiple times under different names or in different cropping seasons. Verification today relies heavily on manual field surveys and self reported crop cutting experiments, which do not scale to the volume of claims filed every season and leave a lot of room for claims to slip through unverified or under scrutinized.
This is a real financial leakage problem for insurers and for the government subsidy that backs the scheme, and it also hurts genuine farmers indirectly, since high fraud rates make insurers more conservative about payouts and pricing across the board.

### 2. Why This Approach Is Different
Most hackathon projects that touch agriculture default to crop disease detection from leaf images or a chatbot for scheme information, both of which are heavily saturated categories at SIH. This project instead targets claims verification, which is a less common angle and maps directly to money, which tends to land better with judges from insurance or agriculture ministry backgrounds.
The core idea is to cross check what a claim says against independent, objective, publicly available signals: satellite observed crop health and radar backscatter over the claimed period, and actual rainfall during that period from IMD and Open-Meteo data. If a claim says drought caused total crop loss but the satellite record shows a healthy canopy through the claimed window and rainfall was within normal range, that is a strong, explainable mismatch. This reuses the same embed, cluster, explain pipeline shape used in an earlier RCA and anomaly detection project, applied to a completely different domain, which keeps the technical risk low while the application is genuinely underexplored at the student hackathon level. As implemented for this MVP, the "cluster" step in that shape is a transparent deterministic rules engine rather than density based clustering — see Section 5.2 for why.

### 3. What This System Does Not Do
This needs to be stated clearly up front, because it shapes every design decision downstream. The system does not label a claim as fraudulent, and it never auto approves or auto rejects a claim. It produces a signal comparison and a plain language explanation of what does or does not match between the claim and the satellite and weather record, and routes that to a human insurance surveyor for final review. A false positive here means a genuine farmer's claim gets flagged for extra scrutiny, which is a real cost to a person who may already be in financial distress. That is a meaningful harm, so the system is built to assist a reviewer, not to replace one, and the confidence bar for anything approaching an automatic flag is set high on purpose.

### 4. Target Users
Primary users are claims processing staff at insurance companies empanelled under PMFBY, and state level agriculture department officials who oversee scheme implementation and are also trying to control leakage. Secondary beneficiaries are farmers themselves, since a verification layer that catches fraudulent claims faster in theory supports faster, more confident payouts for genuine ones, though this project does not attempt to prove that downstream effect within hackathon scope.

### 5. System Overview
The system has three components for this MVP: a satellite and weather signal extraction step, a deterministic signal comparison step, and an explanation layer, followed by a routing step to human review.

#### 5.1 Satellite and Weather Signal Extraction
For a given claim, the system pulls the claimed plot's location and the claimed loss period, then retrieves Sentinel-2 NDVI and NDWI tiles for that plot covering a window before, during, and after the claimed loss event, via the Copernicus Data Space API. NDVI tracks vegetation health and NDWI tracks water content in vegetation, and together they give a reasonable picture of whether a visible crop health decline actually happened during the claimed period.
Cloud cover is a real problem with optical satellite imagery and cannot be ignored, and it is the dominant case rather than an edge case: during the Kharif monsoon season, the large majority of village tiles in the pilot dataset came back more than 50 percent cloudy. Any tile above that threshold is flagged unusable via an explicit `ndvi_reliable` flag rather than treated as a valid healthy or unhealthy reading. Sentinel-1 VV/VH SAR backscatter is retrieved for the same plot and window as a cloud penetrating fallback, using progressive window widening (plus or minus 3, 6, then 9 days) to maximize the chance of a usable pass, and the VH/VV delta is used in place of NDVI whenever `ndvi_reliable` is false.
Alongside this, the system pulls rainfall data for the claimed district and period, primarily from Open-Meteo with IMD as a supplementary source, to check whether the claimed cause, most commonly drought or excess rainfall, is consistent with what the weather record actually shows for that location and time window.

#### 5.2 Deterministic Signal Comparison (revised from HDBSCAN clustering)
The original design called for embedding claim narratives and structured metadata, then clustering claims with HDBSCAN to separate signal-consistent claims from mismatched ones, mirroring the anomaly detection approach used in an earlier RCA pipeline. In implementation, density based clustering needs a claim volume this MVP's pilot dataset does not have, and a rules engine is more directly explainable to a non-technical reviewer or judge, so this MVP uses explicit deterministic thresholds instead: for example an NDVI or SAR delta beyond a set magnitude, or a rainfall deficit beyond a set percentage, in the direction that contradicts the claim narrative. Each flag traces to a specific number crossing a specific, stated threshold, rather than to a cluster assignment a reviewer would have to trust without inspecting it. HDBSCAN based clustering remains a natural extension once claim volume is large enough to make density based separation meaningful, and is carried forward as future scope rather than current scope (Section 12).
A second, independent safeguard sits above the per-signal comparison: where a required ground-truth dimension cannot be sourced and verified from a citable, traceable dataset, that dimension is explicitly marked `NO_DATA` rather than estimated, backfilled, or averaged from a mismatched granularity. For this MVP, state drought declarations (KSDMA) lacked a verifiable public source and were dropped entirely rather than used unverified, and district-level historical yield baselines (DES) were unavailable at matching granularity, so those baselines are also marked `NO_DATA` rather than compared against a coarser state-level average that would bias the result. The consequence is stated plainly rather than hidden: a four-signal composite verdict that depends on both of these dimensions currently resolves to `INCONCLUSIVE` for the full pilot set, and the two-signal comparison in Section 5.1 (satellite plus rainfall) is what actually flags `MISMATCH` versus `INCONCLUSIVE` for the demo. This is treated as a design feature, not a shortfall: the system is built to refuse a confident verdict when it cannot back that verdict with verified data, rather than to fill the gap with a plausible-looking number.
Duplicate claim detection, comparing plot geometry, location, and claim metadata across the claim database to catch the same physical plot claimed twice under different names or overlapping seasons, was part of the original exploratory scope. It is not implemented in this MVP and is not demoed live; it remains future scope (Section 12), to be built once cadastral geometry data for the pilot region is integrated into the pipeline rather than only used as a boundary reference.

#### 5.3 Explanation Layer
Once a claim's signals are compared against its narrative, the system generates a plain language explanation of the result, in terms an insurance reviewer can immediately act on, for example noting that the claim cites drought for a specific window but district rainfall during that window was within the normal range and satellite or radar signal shows the canopy remained largely intact.
This explanation is generated from already computed, deterministic numbers: the NDVI or SAR delta, and the rainfall delta against normal range. The language model's role, via the Groq API, is limited to phrasing these numbers into readable, scheme-relevant language. It does not reason freely over raw satellite data or make its own judgment about whether a claim is suspicious; that judgment comes entirely from the threshold comparison step upstream, which keeps the actual determination logic transparent and auditable rather than buried inside a model's free text output. Where API rate limits are hit mid-run, the pipeline falls back to a deterministic templated explanation for the affected rows rather than dropping them, and each explanation is tagged as model-generated or template-generated so the distinction is visible to a reviewer, never silently blended.

### 6. Human Review Routing
Every claim's comparison result, the specific signals behind it, and the plain language explanation are surfaced to a human reviewer view. Nothing in this system results in an automatic claim rejection or automatic payout denial, and nothing in this system auto-labels a claim as fraudulent. This is the same design principle used in an earlier clinical-domain PRD's safety gate, applied here for a different but equally serious reason, since an incorrect automatic denial has direct financial consequences for a farmer.

### 7. Data Sources
Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 imagery are accessed through the Copernicus Data Space API, which offers sufficient free tier request volume for a hackathon prototype working with a limited number of claims and plots. Open-Meteo provides the primary rainfall data source, with IMD gridded rainfall data as a supplementary source. Bhoomi, Karnataka's cadastral record system, provides the plot boundary layer used to scope the pilot region. None of these require a paid subscription for the scope of this project.

### 8. Technology Stack
*   **Satellite data retrieval:** Sentinel-1/Sentinel-2 imagery through the Copernicus Data Space API.
*   **Weather data:** Open-Meteo, with IMD gridded rainfall data as a supplementary source.
*   **Signal comparison:** a deterministic threshold-based rules engine over NDVI/SAR deltas and rainfall deltas, in place of the originally proposed sentence-transformers embedding plus HDBSCAN clustering pipeline. Embedding-based clustering is retained as future scope once claim volume supports it (Section 12).
*   **Explanation generation:** Groq, used only for phrasing, not for the underlying comparison result.
*   **Backend and pipeline:** Python, FastAPI.
*   **Data layer:** Supabase (Postgres) for the evaluated demo dataset.
*   **Frontend:** a Next.js dashboard for the reviewer view, showing each claim's comparison result, underlying signal values, and explanation, deployable on Vercel's free tier for the demo.
*   **Cadastral reference:** Bhoomi, Karnataka.

### 9. Minimum Viable Scope for the Hackathon
The MVP is scoped to Karnataka only, using the Bhoomi cadastral layer to define the pilot village set, rather than a system claiming national scale. This means pulling real Sentinel-1/2 and rainfall data for a pilot set of real plot locations across roughly 100 villages, pairing them with a set of synthetic PMFBY claim narratives layered on top of those real satellite and rainfall anchors, some designed to genuinely match the record and some designed to mismatch it, and running the full pipeline end to end on that set. Synthetic ground-truth fraud labels are used deliberately and disclosed as such, since verified public fraud-label data for PMFBY claims does not exist; the satellite and rainfall anchors underneath the synthetic claims are real.
Duplicate detection is out of scope for this MVP demo (see Section 5.2) and is not claimed as a working or demoed feature.

### 10. Demo Script Outline
*   Open with the scale of the PMFBY leakage problem, backed by a public statistic if one is available, to establish this is a real, funded, actively discussed problem rather than a hypothetical one.
*   Walk through a claim that matches the satellite and rainfall record, showing a low-mismatch result and a short confirming explanation, to establish the system does not just flag everything.
*   Walk through a claim that does not match, showing the NDVI/SAR and rainfall values alongside the generated plain language explanation, and the resulting flag surfaced in the reviewer view.
*   Show the `NO_DATA` safeguard directly: pull up a claim where the four-signal composite is `INCONCLUSIVE` because KSDMA and DES ground truth could not be verified, and walk through why that is a deliberate refusal to hallucinate a verdict rather than a gap being hidden.
*   Close by restating clearly that the system never auto rejects or auto labels a claim, every result goes to a human, and walk through why that matters given the real cost of a false positive to a farmer.

### 11. Known Limitations
Cloud cover on optical satellite imagery limits how reliable NDVI readings are for most of the Kharif monsoon window in this pilot region, which is why SAR fallback is the primary signal path rather than an edge-case backup for the majority of villages in-season. Two of the four originally planned ground-truth dimensions, state drought declarations and historical yield baselines, are unverified at usable granularity for this pilot and are marked `NO_DATA` rather than estimated, which limits the four-signal composite verdict to `INCONCLUSIVE` for the current pilot set; the two-signal satellite-plus-rainfall comparison is the actionable output for this MVP. Duplicate claim detection based on plot geometry is not implemented in this MVP. NDVI and rainfall data also cannot capture every legitimate cause of crop loss, such as pest attacks or localized hail damage that may not show up clearly in either signal, so the system is a screening layer that narrows down what a human reviewer should look at more closely, not a complete substitute for field verification. The pilot dataset uses synthetic claim narratives over real satellite and rainfall anchors, since verified public PMFBY fraud-label data does not exist, and this should be stated plainly rather than implied to be real claims data.

### 12. Future Scope
Sourcing a verifiable, citable drought-declaration dataset and yield baseline at matching granularity so the four-signal composite verdict can move beyond `INCONCLUSIVE`. Implementing plot-geometry based duplicate claim detection once cadastral geometry is integrated into the pipeline. Revisiting embedding-based clustering (sentence-transformers plus HDBSCAN) once claim volume is large enough for density-based separation to be meaningful, as originally scoped. Expanding coverage beyond Karnataka as better digitized land record data becomes available in other states. Integrating with insurer claim management systems so results and explanations flow directly into existing reviewer workflows rather than a standalone dashboard.

### 13. Summary
This is a claims verification support system for PMFBY, not an automatic fraud determination system. It cross checks synthetic farmer claim narratives against real, independently sourced satellite (optical and radar) and rainfall data for a Karnataka pilot, flags claims where the narrative does not match the observed signals using an explainable deterministic rules engine, and refuses to produce a composite verdict where required ground truth cannot be verified rather than filling the gap with an estimate. Every result goes to a human reviewer, and the system is explicit about where its signals are reliable and where they are not — including where planned components (HDBSCAN clustering, duplicate geometry detection, two of four ground-truth dimensions) are not yet implemented — which is a stronger and more honest position to defend in front of judges than a system that overstates its own completeness.
